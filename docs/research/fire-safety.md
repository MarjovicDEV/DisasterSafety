# Fire Safety for Grade 3-6 Learners (Philippines)

Date: 2026-11-26

Purpose: Source-backed fire safety facts for the game's Fire Episode (Levels at home, at school and in public places, in Baluan, General Santos City). Every finding has a stable ID (FI-xx) so game text can cite it.

## How this was gathered

- Primary sources first: BFP (Bureau of Fire Protection), DepEd, the Philippine Government (laws, proclamations, government news), then NFPA, USFA, the American Red Cross and Ready.gov.
- bfp.gov.ph could not be read live. Every page returned HTTP 403 and a "Performing security verification" bot check. The check was not bypassed. The BFP "Standardized Public Fire Education Manual" volumes [S1] [S2] [S3] were read from Internet Archive (Wayback Machine) copies of the official bfp.gov.ph PDF files. These are copies of the BFP files, not a third-party rewrite. The PDFs have real text; text was extracted and read.
- [S1] (BFP, Volume 1) is the main Philippine source for learners. It has lesson plans for preschool and primary school, with a story, a "S.A.F.E." lesson and a Yes/No activity.
- DepEd PDFs were downloaded and text was extracted. DO 53, s. 2022 [S5] is a scanned PDF with poor text recognition. Only clear lines are used.
- The DepEd DRRR Grade 12 module [S4] was read from a third-party mirror (depedtambayan.net). The DepEd LRMDS portal needs a login. The DepEd Grade 4 Health "Kiddie Fire Marshal" modules on LRMDS were found but could not be downloaded (login required).
- NFPA web pages load their text with JavaScript. They were read in a browser. The NFPA "Educational Messages Desk Reference, 2026 Edition" [S11] was downloaded as a PDF and its text was extracted. It has message lists by grade (Kindergarten to Grade 2).
- USFA, Red Cross and Ready.gov pages were fetched as HTML and read as text. Red Cross fact sheets were read as PDF text. One USFA fact sheet [S17] is no longer on usfa.fema.gov; it was read from a Wayback copy.
- ndrrmc.gov.ph and ocd.gov.ph returned HTTP 403. The PIA site (pia.gov.ph and mirror.pia.gov.ph) refused the connection or returned HTTP 403. officialgazette.gov.ph returned HTTP 403. No NDRRMC, OCD or PIA lines are quoted.
- Proclamation No. 115-A and RA 9514 were read from lawphil.net (a third-party law library), because officialgazette.gov.ph was blocked.
- Fire statistics from BFP for 2024 to 2026 were only found in commercial news. These are listed in the last section, not as findings.

## 1. Official core wording

- FI-01: BFP teaches primary learners the acronym "S.A.F.E." for what to do in a fire. [S1] "S- Sound the alarm" "A- Call 911" "F- Fight the small fire using a fire extinguisher" "E- Escape and be safe." In the story the letters are also given as "A - Alert the Authorities / Call 911." and "F - Fight the Fire". See FI-63 for the child and fire extinguisher problem.
- FI-02: BFP's own words for moving in smoke: "crawl down on your knees" (story), "Fall and crawl" (Yes/No activity for children), and "Crawl low on the floor" (home drill). [S1] "EVACUATE the place by calming yourself, if you need to, crawl down on your knees" [S1] (Yes/No card, answer "YES!") "Fall and crawl" [S3] "Crawl low on the floor."
- FI-03: BFP uses "STOP, DROP, and ROLL" for clothes on fire. [S1] "ask them to STOP! DROP! and ROLL!" [S2] "the first thing they should do is to perform the STOP, DROP, and ROLL!"
- FI-04: BFP uses "go out, stay out" and "once you are out, stay out". [S1] "do not stay inside the burning place; go out, stay out!" and "And remember, once you are out, stay out."
- FI-05: International wording is the same in meaning. [S18] "GET OUT, STAY OUT and CALL FOR HELP." [S11] (home drill) "Get low and go. Get out fast." [S19] "get low and go under the smoke to your exit."
- FI-06: Recommended game wording (core phrases):
  - **"Get out, stay out, call 911."** (FI-04, FI-05, FI-77)
  - **"Crawl low under the smoke."** BFP's child card says "Fall and crawl" (FI-02). "Get low and go" is the NFPA and Red Cross form (FI-05). Use "Crawl low" as the main line, because BFP uses "crawl" in all three places and "crawl low" in [S3].
  - **"Stop, Drop, and Roll."** Add "Cover your face with your hands." (FI-25)
  - **"Feel the door first."** (FI-21)
  - **"Tell an adult."** for matches, lighters and candles (FI-56).
  - "S.A.F.E." can be shown as BFP's school acronym, but the game must not show the child using a fire extinguisher (FI-63, FI-64).

## 2. Get out, stay out, call for help

- FI-07: When there is a fire, leave right away. Do not stay inside. [S1] "Immediately escape/evacuate the burning place. Once you are out, do not go back." [S1] (Yes/No card, answer "YES!") "Go outside"
- FI-08: Never go back inside for people, pets or things. [S11] "Get out and stay out. Never go back inside for people, pets, or things." [S18] "Never go back inside for anything or anyone."
- FI-09: Do not stop to pick up toys or pets. [S11] (message for kindergarten) "Get yourself outside quickly. Do not stop to pick up toys or pets."
- FI-10: Leave your things. Do not delay to collect belongings. [S19] "Leave all your things where they are and save yourself." [S4] "Do not delay your exit trying to collect your belongings."
- FI-11: Only firefighters go into a burning building. Tell them who or what is still inside. [S20] "Only professional firefighters should enter a building that is on fire" [S12] "Firefighters have the skills and equipment to perform rescues." [S11] "If pets are trapped inside your home, tell firefighters right away."
- FI-12: Shout "Fire!" to warn others. [S19] "Yell "Fire!" several times and go outside right away." [S4] "Sound the fire alarm. SHOUT "FIRE! FIRE! FIRE!""
- FI-13: Go to the outside meeting place. Wait there. [S11] (kindergarten) "Wait at your outside meeting place with your family." [S1] (Yes/No card, answer "YES!") "Find the meeting spot/ safe spot"
- FI-14: Wait in a safe area for help. Do not go back until an adult or responder says it is safe. [S1] "Look for a safe area where you can rest and wait for the authorities" [S4] "Do not return inside the building until told to do so."

## 3. Smoke: crawl low, and why smoke kills

- FI-15: Smoke and toxic gases kill more people than flames. [S15] "Smoke and toxic gases kill more people than flames do." [S2] "Most people who die in a fire die from toxic gases, thick smoke, and lack of oxygen."
- FI-16: Fire grows very fast. There is little time to escape. [S23] "In less than 30 seconds a small flame can turn into a major fire." [S15] "Residents could have less than 2 minutes to escape a home fire once the smoke alarm sounds." [S18] "you may have as little as two minutes to escape"
- FI-17: Smoke goes up. Cleaner, cooler air is near the floor. Stay low until you reach the exit. [S3] "Oxygen may still be available at least 1 foot from the ground." and "Do not get up, as you may inhale the toxic products of combustion." [S23] "Heavy smoke and poisonous gases collect first along the ceiling."
- FI-18: Crawl low under smoke to the exit. [S23] "Drop down to the floor and crawl low, under any smoke to your exit." [S17] "Show children how to crawl low on the floor, below the smoke, to get out of the house."
- FI-19: Smoke makes it dark. Heat is a bigger danger than you think. [S15] "Heat is more threatening than flames." [S2] "The thick, black smoke from a fire can make it extremely difficult to see where one is going."
- FI-20: BFP adds: cover the nose. BFP's heavy-smoke acronym "G.C.C.A.I." [S3] "Get a mask or wet towel." "Cover your nose from thick smoke." "Crawl low on the floor." "Anticipate for a zero visibility." "Immediately exit the house." In the BFP school drill game, learners cover their noses with a cloth. [S2] "Cover their noses with a handkerchief/any piece of cloth"

## 4. Doors, and what to do if trapped

- FI-21: Feel the door before you open it. If it is hot, do not open it. Use another way out. [S14] (to children) "Before you open a door, feel it. If it is hot, use your other exit." [S11] "Before opening a door, feel the doorknob and then the space around the door." [S19] "Never open doors that are warm to the touch."
- FI-22: Use the back of the hand to feel the door. [S4] "Feel the door with the back of your hand (more nerve endings, more sensitive to touch)." Only this DepEd module says "back of the hand". NFPA, USFA and Ready.gov say "feel" only.
- FI-23: Close doors behind you as you leave. A closed door slows smoke and fire. [S11] "Close doors behind you as you leave." [S12] "Closing doors on your way out slows the spread of fire, giving you more time to safely escape."
- FI-24: If you cannot get out: stay in the room, close the door, block the gaps with cloth, call for help, and signal at the window. [S11] "close the door and stuff towels or bedding in the cracks around the doors and vents to help keep smoke out." and "Then signal for help at the window with a flashlight or a lightcolored cloth." [S19] "Place a wet towel under the door and call the fire department or 9-1-1." [S4] "Call BFP and tell them exactly where you are located."
  - Do not break the window. [S22] "Open the window- but do not break it." [S4] "do not break it, you may need to close the window if smoke rushes in."
  - BFP says "Jump through the window" is a "NO!" for children. [S1] (Yes/No card, answer "NO!") "Jump through the window"
  - Do not take videos or pictures while trapped. [S1] (Yes/No card, answer "NO!") "Take videos and pictures while trapped in a burning place."

## 5. Clothes on fire, and burns

- FI-25: If your clothes catch fire: stop, drop, cover your face with your hands, and roll until the flames are out. [S11] "Stop where you are, drop to the ground, cover your face with your hands, and roll over and over or back and forth until the flames are out." [S11] (Grade 1 message) "Drop to the ground-lay flat with your legs out straight and cover your eyes and mouth with your hands."
- FI-26: Do not run. Running makes the fire burn faster. [S19] "Running will only make the fire burn faster." [S4] "DO NOT RUN. It will spread the fire across your body."
- FI-27: Use "stop, drop, and roll" only when clothes are on fire. Stay away from fire first. [S11] (Grade 1) "Use "stop, drop, and roll" only if your clothes are on fire."
- FI-28: After the flames are out, get an adult. The adult cools the burn and gets medical help. [S11] (Grade 1 and 2) "Get help from a grown-up who will cool the burn and get medical help"
- FI-29: Cool a burn with cool water for 3 to 5 minutes. Tell an adult right away. [S14] (to children) "If you get burned, put the burn under cool water for 3 to 5 minutes. Tell an adult right away about your burn." [S19] "cool the burned skin with water for three to five minutes."
- FI-30: Do not put creams, sprays or home remedies on a burn. Cover it with a clean, dry cloth. [S11] "Cover burns with a clean, dry cloth. Do not apply creams, ointments, sprays, or other home remedies."
- Note: no primary source read names ice, butter or toothpaste. "Home remedies" [S11] covers them. See the last section.

## 6. Smoke alarms, home escape plan, home drills

- FI-31: A smoke alarm makes a loud "beep, beep, beep" sound. When it sounds, get outside and stay outside. [S11] (Grade 1) "It will make a loud "beep, beep, beep (pause), beep, beep, beep (pause)" sound if there is smoke." and (preschool) "When you hear the smoke alarm, get outside and stay outside"
- FI-32: Children must know the sound of the smoke alarm and what to do. [S20] "Teach your children what smoke alarms sound like and what to do when they hear one." [S3] "Test the smoke alarm in the presence of most family members to make them familiar with the sound."
- FI-33: Test smoke alarms every month (adult task). [S18] "Test smoke alarms every month."
- FI-34: Know two ways out of every room. One can be a window. [S11] (Grade 1) "Every room needs two ways out." and "One way out would be the door and the second way out might be a window." [S3] "Draw a floor plan of your home and find at least 2 ways out of every room."
- FI-35: BFP calls the home fire drill "EDITH" (Exit Drill In The Home). Its steps: [S3]
  - "1. Sound the alarm"
  - "2. Perform crawling low on the floor (if necessary)."
  - "3. Follow the escape route."
  - "4. Exit through the door or to its designated means of egress ."
  - "5. Proceed to safe evacuation area."
  - "6. Conduct headcount."
- FI-36: Practice the home drill at least twice a year. BFP also suggests once every quarter. [S3] "Conduct EDITH consistently preferably at least twice a year." and "it is best to hold the drill at least once every quarter of the year." [S12] "Practice your home fire escape plan twice a year"
- FI-37: The meeting place is outside, in front of the home, at something that does not move (a tree, a post, a neighbor's house). [S11] "Have an outside meeting place (something permanent like a tree, light pole, or mailbox)" [S1] (flip book activity) "Suggestions include a neighbor's house or a tree"
- FI-38: Drills are to practice, not to frighten. A child practices with an adult. [S12] "The objective is to practice, not to frighten" and "Children should only practice with a grown-up, and only from a first-story window."
- FI-39: BFP asks each home to name a "Fire Safety Conscious Person" (an adult) who leads the drill. [S3] "It can be the father, mother, or anyone who is fire safety conscious."
- FI-40: BFP warns that window grills on upper floors can trap people. [S3] "it is not advisable to install grills on its windows, or else it is considered a fire trap."

## 7. School fire drills in the Philippines

- FI-41: All public schools must hold unannounced earthquake and fire drills in the first and third week of every month. [S5] "all public schools are hereby required to conduct unannounced earthquake and fire drills every first and third week of every month." (Clean reading of a scanned page; see the last section.)
- FI-42: When the alarm sounds: stop, listen to the teacher, go quickly and quietly to the meeting place outside, and stay with your class. The teacher takes attendance. [S11] (Grade 1) "(A) Stop what you are doing. (B) Listen for instructions from your teacher or principal. (C) Go quickly and quietly to your meeting place outside the school." and "Your teacher will take attendance to make sure all students are safe."
- FI-43: Walk quickly. Do not run. Stay calm. [S4] "Walk quickly but don't run. ALWAYS STAY CALM." [S11] (preschool) "get up and walk. Do not run, but walk quickly."
- FI-44: The BFP school drill game: cover the nose, leave in a single file, calm and orderly, no running, headcount at the evacuation area. [S2] "Leave the building in a single file and in a calm and orderly manner (No one must run or break the file formation)." and "wait for the team leader to perform the body count"
- FI-45: Use the nearest fire exit and the stairs, not the elevator. Go to the evacuation area. Do not go home. [S4] "Use the nearest fire exit." "Do not use elevators. Always use emergency stairs." "Assemble at the fire evacuation area" and "Do not go home. Rescuers need to ensure that all occupants are safely out of the building & are accounted for."
- FI-46: School staff (not learners) form the School Emergency Response Team (SERT). Learners do not fight the fire. [S3] "in no case shall students be involved in any form of reactionary activity related to the plan." and "The teaching and nonteaching staffs of schools, school administrators and school employees should compose this team."
- FI-47: The school evacuation plan is posted in every classroom. Classroom doors are closed after evacuation. [S3] "The output of the FERPS should be posted in every class room" and "closing of all classroom once evacuated to prevent further spread of fire"
- FI-48: Fire drill compared with earthquake drill. BFP's school guide treats them as two activities. The earthquake drill keeps "Duck, Cover and Hold". The fire simulation follows R.A.C.E.: someone sees the fire and alerts everyone by shouting or knocking on classroom doors, then the alarm, evacuation, and firefighting by staff and firefighters. [S3] "This includes the observance of DUCK, COVER AND HOLD." (earthquake drill) and "R.A.C.E. or Rescue, Activate, Confine and Extinguish." and "The fire informant's job is to alert everyone within the immediate vicinity that there is a fire through shouting or knocking on classroom doors." No fire drill step in any source read uses Duck, Cover and Hold. BFP also says fires can follow earthquakes. [S3] "in the real scenario, earthquakes are always followed by fires."
- Evaluation items for drills: [S3] "Evacuees able to reach the evacuation area unhurt and in order." and "Evacuees are well accounted for."

## 8. Kitchen: stove, grease fire, LPG

- FI-49: Children stay away from the stove. Keep a "safety zone" of 3 feet (1 meter) around it. [S11] "Have a "safety zone" of at least 3 feet (1 meter) around the stove" [S11] (Grade 2) "Only grown-ups should be around items that are hot or could get hot." [S14] "Keep children at least 3 feet away from stoves, heaters or anything that gets hot." BFP's preschool card "Play near the stove." is a thumbs-down item. [S1] "Thumbs down! Please don't do it!"
- FI-50: Unattended cooking starts kitchen fires. Do not leave the stove on. [S1] (Coco story summary) "When cooking, you need to keep looking." and "Do not leave the stove running." [S11] "The leading cause of fires in the kitchen is unattended cooking."
- FI-51: Grease or oil fire in a pan: never use water. Slide a lid over the pan and turn off the heat (adult action). Leave the lid on. [S3] "Never pour water into the burning greases or oils of the pry fan." [S13] "slide the lid over the pan and turn off the burner." and "Never throw water on the fire." [S11] "Never pour water on a cooking pan grease fire." [S16] "Keep a pan lid or baking sheet nearby."
- FI-52: If the kitchen fire does not go out, get everyone out and call from outside. [S13] "get everyone out of your home. Call the fire department from outside." [S3] "Remember the most important thing is to get out of the house safely."
- FI-53: If you smell LPG (gas) or think there is a leak, BFP says: [S3]
  - "Put out all open flames in the area immediately"
  - "Refrain from using the phone or activating any electrical switches, as these actions may trigger sparks"
  - "Widening all windows and doors will help disperse the gas"
  - "turn off the gas supply at the pressure regulator switch" (adult action)
  - "Avoid ringing doorbells"
  - "Evacuate the area promptly, and communicate the urgency to your neighbors"
  - NFPA agrees: leave and call from outside. [S11] "If you smell gas, do not light the appliance. Leave the building immediately"
  - For a child, the game action is: do not touch switches, tell an adult, and get out. No source gives child-only LPG steps (see the last section).

## 9. Electrical fires and overloaded outlets

- FI-54: "Octopus" connections (many plugs in one outlet) overload the outlet and can start a fire. Use one outlet for one appliance. [S3] "Fire may cause overloading due to octopus connection and plugging several appliances into one outlet." and "it is ideal to follow one convenience outlet for one appliance." BFP's children's story covers "octopus wiring, socket overloading". [S1]
- FI-55: Never use water on an electrical fire. It can electrocute you. [S3] "Do not use water to put out the fire, as it can electrocute you or further spread the fire." Damaged cords must be replaced, not repaired at home. [S11] "Replace cracked, damaged, and loose electrical or extension cords. Do not try to repair them." [S23] "Frayed wires can cause fires."

## 10. Candles, mosquito coils, matches and lighters

- FI-56: Never touch matches, lighters or candles. If you find matches or lighters, tell an adult. [S14] (to children) "Never touch matches, lighters or candles. If you find matches or lighters, tell an adult." [S11] "Teach young children to never touch lighters or matches and to tell a grown-up immediately if they find them." [S21] "tell you or a responsible adult when they find matches or lighters at home or school."
- FI-57: Matches and lighters are tools for adults. Fire moves fast and hurts. [S11] "Explain that fire moves very fast and can hurt as soon as it touches them. Tell them that this is why lighters and matches are tools for adults only."
- FI-58: BFP tells children not to play with matches and to stay away from hot things. [S1] "Once they see us playing matches, they say be careful! It is a match; do not play with it." and "A child must be away from anything that gets hot - a match, a stove, a candle, and a fire"
- FI-59: Many home fires start when children play with lighters and matches. [S20] "many home fires are started by children playing with dangerous household items – especially lighters and matches." [S3] (BFP list of common causes) "Children Playing With Fire"

## Why the wrong choices are unsafe

### Hiding under the bed or in the closet

- BFP lists "Hide" as a "NO!" for children (FI-71, FI-75). [S1]
- Ready.gov and USFA tell adults to teach children not to hide from firefighters, and to get out quickly instead (FI-71). [S23] [S17]
- Smoke fills a room fast and kills more people than flames (FI-15, FI-16). A hiding child stays in the smoke. (This link is an inference from FI-15 and FI-16. No primary source read says it in one sentence.)
- Note: "firefighters cannot find a hidden child" is given as the reason only in non-primary sources (see the last section).

### Going back in for toys, pets or things

- Never go back inside for people, pets or things (FI-08). Do not stop for toys or pets (FI-09). Leave your things (FI-10). [S11] [S18] [S19]
- There may be less than 2 minutes to escape (FI-16). [S15]
- Firefighters do rescues. Tell them about pets or people still inside (FI-11). [S11] [S12]
- BFP: "Once you are out, do not go back." (FI-07). [S1]

### Opening a hot door

- A hot door or doorknob means fire may be on the other side. Keep it closed and use another way out (FI-21). [S14] [S11] [S19]
- Feel it with the back of the hand (FI-22). [S4]
- If there is smoke coming around the door, also keep it closed. [S11] "If there is smoke coming in from around the door, leave the door closed and use your second way out."
- If there is no other way out, stay in the room and seal the gaps (FI-24).

### Using the elevator

- Use the stairs, not the elevator (FI-45, FI-68). [S4] [S11] [S19]
- DepEd's DRRR pretest asks what to do when escaping a building fire. The only option that matches the module's lesson is the elevator one. [S4] "D. Don't use the elevators or escape to the roof." (The answer key was not in the copy read.)
- Note: no primary source read gives the reason (for example, power cut or the shaft filling with smoke). See the last section.

### Standing up or running through smoke

- Smoke and hot gases collect at the ceiling. Cleaner, cooler air is near the floor (FI-17). [S3] [S23]
- Standing up means you may breathe in toxic smoke. [S3] "Do not get up, as you may inhale the toxic products of combustion."
- Smoke and toxic gases kill more people than flames (FI-15). [S15] [S2]
- Running is not allowed in drills (FI-43, FI-44). [S4] [S2]

### Throwing water on a grease (oil) fire

- Never put water on burning oil (FI-51). [S3] [S13] [S11]
- BFP's manual shows this as a wrong action. [S3] (picture caption) "It shows someone attempting to extinguish a fire from burning oil by using water."
- Note: no primary source read explains why in words (water makes the burning oil splash and spread). See the last section.

### Running when clothes catch fire

- Running makes the fire burn faster and spreads it on the body (FI-26). [S19] [S4]
- Stop, drop, cover the face, and roll (FI-25). [S11] [S23]
- DepEd's DRRR assessment asks what to do if your clothing catches fire. Its options include "Run for help." and "Try to put it out with your hands." The option that matches the module's lesson is: [S4] "D. Stop, drop, and roll while covering your face." (The answer key was not in the copy read.)

### Panicking or freezing

- Stay calm, then act. [S4] "ALWAYS STAY CALM." [S3] "Stay calm and don't panic" [S1] "EVACUATE the place by calming yourself"
- BFP school drills aim to reduce panic. [S3] "in an orderly and safe manner reducing panic"
- Fire grows fast, so waiting is dangerous (FI-16). [S23] [S15]
- BFP's story accepts that children feel scared or cry. [S1] (child's possible answer) "I will be scared." The answer is to practice drills so the child knows what to do (FI-36, FI-41). [S12] "The objective is to practice, not to frighten"
- Note: no primary source read names "freezing" as a behavior.

### Playing with matches, lighters or candles

- Many home fires start this way (FI-59). [S20] [S3]
- Matches and lighters are tools for adults only (FI-57). [S11]
- If you find them, do not touch them; tell an adult (FI-56). [S14] [S11] [S21]
- BFP: "do not play with it" (FI-58). [S1]
- About half of child-playing fires start in a bedroom. [S21] "Just over half of child-playing fires in the home start in a bedroom" (US data.)

## Game mapping (candidates)

Candidate situations only. These are not Level designs.

### Home

- **Kitchen, pan of oil catches fire (adult cooking).**
  - Safe Choice: shout "Fire!", tell the adult, step away from the stove, get out with the family; the adult covers the pan with a lid and turns off the gas only if it is small. FI-12, FI-49, FI-51, FI-52, FI-07.
  - Unsafe Choices: throw a cup of water on the pan (FI-51); try to carry the pan outside (FI-51, [S11] "Do not move the pan."); stay and watch (FI-16, FI-07).
- **Kitchen, child alone, stove left on, pot boiling over (BFP "Coco" story).**
  - Safe Choice: do not leave the stove; call the adult at once; stay out of the 1-meter zone. FI-50, FI-49.
  - Unsafe Choices: go out to play and leave the stove on (FI-50); put a towel near the flame to "help" (FI-49, [S11] keep things that burn away).
- **Kitchen, LPG smell.**
  - Safe Choice: do not touch switches or the phone inside; tell an adult; open windows and door on the way; go outside; call 911 from outside. FI-53, FI-77.
  - Unsafe Choices: switch on the light to look (FI-53); light a match or the stove (FI-53); stay inside and play (FI-53).
- **Bedroom at night, smoke alarm sounds, smoke in the hallway.**
  - Safe Choice: get out of bed, crawl low, feel the door with the back of the hand, use the second way out if hot, close doors behind, go to the meeting place, stay out. FI-31, FI-18, FI-21, FI-22, FI-34, FI-23, FI-13, FI-08.
  - Unsafe Choices: hide under the bed or in the closet (FI-71); stand up and run through the smoke (FI-17); open the hot door (FI-21); go back for a toy or the cat (FI-08, FI-09).
  - Note: smoke alarms are not common in Philippine homes (unconfirmed; see the last section). An adult shouting "Sunog!" or a neighbor's alarm could be the trigger instead.
- **Bedroom, trapped (door hot, no second way out).**
  - Safe Choice: keep the door closed, put cloth at the door gap, call 911 and say where you are, wave a bright cloth or flashlight at the window. FI-24, FI-77, FI-78.
  - Unsafe Choices: jump from the window (FI-24, FI-75); open the door (FI-21); take videos (FI-24, FI-75).
- **Living room, octopus wiring sparks / small electrical fire.**
  - Safe Choice: do not touch it, shout "Fire!", tell an adult, get out. The adult turns off the main switch. FI-54, FI-55, FI-12.
  - Unsafe Choices: pour water on it (FI-55); pull the plugs by hand (FI-55, adult task); ignore it.
- **Brownout, candle or mosquito coil (katol) near the curtain.**
  - Safe Choice: tell an adult; use a flashlight; keep the candle or katol on a heat-safe surface away from curtains. FI-60, FI-61, FI-62.
  - Unsafe Choices: play with the candle or matches (FI-56, FI-58); leave the candle burning when going to sleep (FI-61).
- **Finds a lighter or matches.**
  - Safe Choice: do not touch; tell an adult. FI-56, FI-57.
  - Unsafe Choices: try to light it (FI-59); hide it in a pocket or under the bed (FI-56).
- **Clothes catch fire (sleeve near the stove, or near a bonfire or "siga").**
  - Safe Choice: stop, drop, cover the face with the hands, roll until the flames are out; then cool the burn with cool water and tell an adult. FI-25, FI-29, FI-28.
  - Unsafe Choices: run for help (FI-26); try to beat the flames with the hands (FI-25, FI-26; [S4] lists "Try to put it out with your hands." as a test option); put toothpaste on the burn (FI-30).
- **Home fire drill (EDITH) with the family.**
  - Safe Choice: follow the escape route, crawl low where needed, go to the meeting place, be counted. FI-35, FI-36, FI-37.

### School

- **Classroom fire drill (alarm rings).**
  - Safe Choice: stop, listen to the teacher, leave the bag, walk quickly in a single file, do not push, use the stairs, go to the evacuation area, stay with the class for the headcount. FI-42, FI-43, FI-44, FI-45, FI-10.
  - Unsafe Choices: do Duck, Cover and Hold (the earthquake action; FI-48); run and push (FI-43, FI-44); go back for the bag (FI-10); go home (FI-45).
- **School canteen, small fire at the stove or oil.**
  - Safe Choice: shout "Fire!", tell the canteen staff or teacher, move away, follow the teacher out. FI-12, FI-46, FI-51.
  - Unsafe Choices: pick up the fire extinguisher (FI-63, FI-46); throw water on the oil (FI-51); stay to watch (FI-16).
- **Hallway full of smoke during a drill or real fire.**
  - Safe Choice: cover the nose with a handkerchief, stay low, follow the teacher to the exit. FI-20, FI-18, FI-44.
  - Unsafe Choices: stand and run (FI-17); go to the comfort room to hide (FI-71).
- **Sees smoke from a classroom first.**
  - Safe Choice: shout "Fire!" and tell the nearest teacher; the teacher sounds the alarm. FI-12, FI-48, FI-76.
  - Unsafe Choices: keep quiet (FI-12); press the alarm as a joke on another day (FI-76).

### Public places

- **Mall or market, fire alarm rings.**
  - Safe Choice: stay with the parent, follow the exit signs and the guards, use the stairs, go to the assembly area or meeting place. FI-66, FI-67, FI-68, FI-69.
  - Unsafe Choices: take the elevator (FI-68); run and push in the crowd (FI-43); go back to the shop for the toy that was bought (FI-08, FI-10).
- **Neighbor's house on fire (barangay).**
  - Safe Choice: stay away, tell an adult, make sure someone calls 911, give the location. FI-77, FI-78, FI-07.
  - Unsafe Choices: go near to watch or film it (FI-16, FI-75); run inside to help (FI-11).
- **Firefighter in full gear comes toward the child.**
  - Safe Choice: go to the firefighter and follow instructions. FI-72, FI-73.
  - Unsafe Choices: run and hide from the firefighter (FI-71, FI-72).
