# Typhoon Safety for Grade 3-6 Learners (Philippines)

Date: 2026-11-26

Purpose: Source-backed typhoon safety facts for the game's Typhoon Episode (Levels before, during and after a typhoon, at home, at school and outside, in Baluan, General Santos City). Every finding has a stable ID (TY-xx) so game text can cite it.

## How this was gathered

- Primary sources first: PAGASA (warnings, flyers, bulletins, climate data), DepEd (orders and a learner module), the Philippine Government (laws), DILG (Operation L!STO manuals), UNICEF Philippines, then Ready.gov (FEMA), NOAA and the American Red Cross.
- Research ran from the evening of 2026-11-26 into 2026-11-27. Each source line gives the day it was read.
- PAGASA pages were read live. The PAGASA "Payong PAGASA" flyers (Rainfall Warning System, Tropical Cyclone Warning System) are images. They were downloaded from the PAGASA publications page and read by eye.
- DepEd Order No. 37, s. 2022 [S12] and DepEd Order No. 22, s. 2024 [S13] are scanned PDFs on deped.gov.ph with no text layer. The page images were extracted and read by eye. Quotes are from clean, printed pages.
- DO 22, s. 2024 [S13] is the newer order. It changed the class suspension rules of DO 37, s. 2022 [S12]. See TY-12 and the conflicts list.
- ndrrmc.gov.ph and ocd.gov.ph returned HTTP 403. No NDRRMC or OCD page was read. One OCD go bag list was read only as relayed by PTV News [S21] (state media).
- dilg.gov.ph news pages failed with a TLS certificate error. The check was not bypassed. The two DILG manuals [S17] [S18] were read from the official download links that the DILG Region 5 "Operation L!STO" page gives (bit.ly links to Google Drive). Both PDFs have real text.
- The WebFetch tool could not open web.archive.org. The Wayback availability API was used once; the archived PAGASA "Rainfall and Thunderstorm Warning" page (2021) had no warning text, so it is not cited.
- American Red Cross pages returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool. They were read as plain HTML with a normal browser user agent.
- The DepEd Grade 4 Science module [S15] was read from a third-party mirror (depedtambayan.net). LRMDS needs a login. It is the only DepEd learner material for Grade 3-6 on typhoons that could be read.
- The Philippine Red Cross site (redcross.org.ph) loads, but no typhoon "what to do" page was found on it. No PRC lines are quoted.
- No IFRC, WHO or DOH page was read as a primary source. Their points are listed in the last section.
- EO 66, s. 2012 and RA 10121 were read from lawphil.net (a third-party law library), as in fire-safety.md.
- American Red Cross and UNICEF pages are copyrighted. Their points are given in plain words, not quoted. Philippine Government works (RA 8293, Sec. 176) and US federal pages (Ready.gov, NOAA) are quoted.

## 1. Warnings and signals

- TY-01: PAGASA warns of typhoon winds with Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (TCWS) 1 to 5. The current 5-level system has been used since 23 March 2022. A higher signal means stronger winds and less time before they arrive. [S1] "has been in use since 23 March 2022" [S2] lead time is "the time between hoisting of wind signal and the onset of the wind threat". Lead times: Signal 1, 36 hours; Signal 2, 24 hours; Signal 3, 18 hours; Signal 4 and 5, 12 hours. [S1] [S2]
  - Unsafe choice: thinking "the wind is still calm, so the signal does not matter" and going out to play. The signal is raised hours before the winds come (36 to 12 hours) so people can get ready [S1].
- TY-02: Signal 1: "Strong winds" of 39 to 61 km/h. [S3] "Minimal to minor threat to life and property." [S1] Banana plants tilt, twigs sway, light-material houses may get small damage.
  - Unsafe choice: skipping preparation because "it is only Signal 1". Signals can jump from 1 to 3 (TY-07).
- TY-03: Signal 2: "Gale-force winds" of 62 to 88 km/h. [S3] "Minor to moderate threat to life and property." Loose light things fly. Some wires fall. [S1] "Unsecures [sic], exposed lightweight items may become projectiles" and "Some electrical wires may be blown down, resulting in local power outages."
  - Unsafe choice: leaving toys, pails, chairs or flower pots in the yard. Signal 2 winds can throw them [S1].
- TY-04: Signal 3: "Storm-force winds" of 89 to 117 km/h. [S3] "Moderate to significant threat to life and property." [S1] roofs, windows and walls of weak houses may be damaged; many areas lose power, with "numerous downed power lines ans [sic] posts"; some big trees fall.
  - Unsafe choice: walking outside under trees and posts during Signal 3. Trees, posts and wires come down [S1].
- TY-05: Signal 4: "Typhoon-force winds" of 118 to 184 km/h. [S3] "Significant to severe threat to life and property." [S1] "Considerable airborne debris willbe [sic] generated and may cause damage, injury, and possible fatalities." Windows of houses are likely to blow out. Power and phone service are almost all lost.
  - Unsafe choice: standing near a window to watch the storm. At Signal 4 windows "are likely to blow out" and flying debris injures people [S1].
- TY-06: Signal 5: "Typhoon-force winds" of 185 km/h or more (super typhoon). [S3] "Extreme threat to life and property." [S1] "People, pets, and livestock exposed to the wind are at great risk of injury or death." Power, water and phone service may be gone for a long time.
  - Unsafe choice: going outside during the storm for any reason, even to check on a pet. People in the open wind are at great risk [S1].
- TY-07: Signals are raised for a province or a city, and can skip levels. [S1] "the escalation or de-escalation of wind signals allows the skipping of wind signal levels" (example on the page: Signal 1 raised straight to Signal 3). A city or municipality can get its own signal. Local winds can be stronger on high ground and near the sea.
  - Unsafe choice: waiting for "Signal 2 first" before getting ready. The next bulletin may already be Signal 3 [S1].
- TY-08: PAGASA rainfall warnings have three colors. [S4] Yellow = "ADVISORY": "Flooding is possible." Orange = "ALERT": "Flooding is alarming." Red = "EMERGENCY": "Serious flooding is damaging." The Heavy Rainfall Warning is "Usually caused by tropical cyclones, monsoons, etc." [S3] gives the same levels as "Community AWARENESS", "Community PREPAREDNESS" and "Community RESPONSE", with the red level text "SEVERE Flooding is EXPECTED. Take necessary precautionary measures."
  - Unsafe choice: thinking rain is "just rain" and going to play near the canal under a yellow or orange warning. Flooding is possible to alarming [S4].
- TY-09: What to do for each rainfall color. [S4]
  - Yellow: "Listen to the radio or watch the news for the latest updates." "Monitor the water levels in rivers and low-lying areas." Impact: "Flooding in low-lying areas especially along river channels".
  - Orange: "Get ready for evacuation with your emergency kit." Impacts: "Flooding in other areas", "Possible landslides".
  - Red: "Evacuate to higher ground and away from landslide prone areas." Impacts: "Widespread flooding", "More roads are not passable", "Higher chance of landslides".
  - Unsafe choice: at red, staying in a low house near the river "to wait and see". PAGASA says evacuate to higher ground [S4].
- TY-10: A storm surge is a rise of the sea during a typhoon. PAGASA issues storm surge warnings with expected heights for coastal towns. [S8] "the abnormal rise in sea level that occurs during tropical cyclones or "bagyo"." [S9] for a surge of "2.1 - 3.0 meters": "Evacuation is advised." "Move to higher grounds away from the coast and storm surge-prone areas." [S5] "STORM SURGES CAN FLOOD LOW-LYING COASTAL COMMUNITIES." "ESPECIALLY WHEN IT IS HIGH TIDE."
  - Unsafe choice: going to the beach to watch the big waves. Storm surges flood low coasts [S5] [S9].
- TY-11: A typhoon brings four hazards: heavy rain and floods, strong winds, storm surge, and landslides. Floods can make drinking water dirty. [S5] "HEAVY RAIN CAN CAUSE FLOODING" "DRINKING WATER MAY BE CONTAMINATED. THIS MAY LEAD TO THE OUTBREAK OF DISEASES." "LANDSLIDES AND MUDFLOWS CAN BURY PEOPLE ALIVE AND DESTROY PROPERTIES." [S25] (in plain words) a hurricane, which is the same storm as a typhoon, brings high winds, heavy rain, storm surge and flooding, and can damage places far inland.
  - Unsafe choice: thinking "we live far from the sea, so the typhoon cannot hurt us". Rain floods and landslides reach inland [S5] [S25].

## 2. Class suspension (no classes)

- TY-12: Current DepEd rule (DO 22, s. 2024): classes stop by themselves under these signals. [S13] "The suspension of classes due to typhoons and tropical cyclones will follow PAGASA's Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (TCWS) and Executive Order No. 66, s. 2012."
  - "TCWS 1": "Classes for Kindergarten in the affected area shall be automatically suspended"
  - "TCWS 2": "Face to face Classes for Kindergarten and Elementary to Junior High School (JHS) or Grade 10 in the affected area shall be automatically suspended" (schools shift to modules, performance tasks, projects or make-up classes).
  - "TCWS 3 or above": "Classes and work in all levels in the affected area shall be automatically suspended."
  - For Grade 3 to 6: no face-to-face classes at Signal 2 or higher. At Signal 1, Grade 3 to 6 classes are not automatically suspended.
  - EO 66 has the same three steps. [S14] "When Signal No. 2 is raised by PAGASA, classes at the pre-school, elementary and secondary levels, in the affected area, shall be automatically cancelled or suspended,"
  - Older rule (DO 37, s. 2022) cancelled classes from Kindergarten to Grade 12 under any signal 1 to 5. See the conflicts list.
  - Unsafe choice: walking to school at Signal 2 because "I do not want to miss class". Classes are already suspended [S13].
- TY-13: If a signal is raised while learners are already at school, the school stops classes and sends everyone home only if it is safe. If travel is not safe, learners stay at school. [S13] "If the TCWS is issued at a time when classes have already begun, the school shall immediately suspend the classes and work and send everyone home, if it is safe to do so. However, schools are obligated to keep the students and personnel safe in school if traveling has become unsafe." DO 37 [S12] has the same rule.
  - Unsafe choice: leaving school alone in the storm when classes stop. The school must keep learners safe at school if travel is unsafe [S13].
- TY-14: Rainfall warnings and class suspension. [S13]
  - Yellow warning: the local chief executive (mayor) decides, with the schools division: "The LCE shall decide on the suspension of face to face classes, and work in schools".
  - Orange or Red warning before the school day: "Classes and work from Kindergarten to Grade 12 and ALS are automatically suspended in schools."
  - Orange or Red warning during the school day: "The school head shall immediately suspend classes and work from Kindergarten to Grade 12 and ALS and send everyone home if it is safe to do so. However, schools are obligated to keep the learners and personnel safely in school if traveling has become unsafe."
  - School heads may also suspend classes "In case of torrential rain, flooding, and risk of landslides and storm surges".
  - Unsafe choice: going to the river after school "because there are no classes anyway". A suspension means stay safe at home, not play outside (TY-34).
- TY-15: Parents and guardians decide if a child goes to school during bad weather, even if classes are not suspended. [S13] "Parents or guardians have the ultimate responsibility for determining whether their children should attend classes in consideration of their physical and/or mental health during disasters and calamities. This applies even if no order for suspension of classes has been issued." DepEd's Grade 4 module asks: under Signal 1, classes not suspended, but your place floods easily. Would you go to school? One answer choice is "I will not go to school because I might be caught in the flood." [S15] (The answer key is an image and could not be read.)
  - Unsafe choice: going to school alone when Mom or Dad says to stay home because the road floods.
- TY-16: Suspensions are announced by the LGU (mayor and the local DRRM office) on radio and TV, early in the morning. [S14] "The concerned Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (LDRRMO) headed by the local chief executive shall be responsible for announcing the suspension of classes" and announcements "not later than 4:30 AM of the day of the intended cancellation of classes". [S14] Without a signal, "localized cancellation or suspension of classes ... may be implemented by local chief executives".
  - Unsafe choice: trusting a classmate's text or a rumor instead of the radio, TV or the school. (The "rumor" part is not in a source; the rule that the LGU announces is.)
- TY-17: Schools can be used as evacuation centers for a short time. [S13] "schools may be used as an immediate evacuation site which should not last for more than 15 days. LGUs cannot use schools as long-term shelters for evacuees."
  - Unsafe choice: none for a child. This fact explains why the Evacuation Area can be a school or a covered court.

## 3. Barangay warnings: who gives them and how

- TY-18: Every barangay has a Barangay Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Committee (BDRRMC), led by the punong barangay (barangay captain). Cities and towns have a DRRM office (CDRRMO/MDRRMO) with a staff member for "operations and warning". [S16] "a Barangay Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Committee (BDRRMC) in every barangay" and "The LDRRMO shall be under the office of the governor, city or municipal mayor, and the punong barangay in case of the BDRRMC." and staff for "(3) operations and warning".
  - Unsafe choice: ignoring the barangay officials' warning because "PAGASA did not call us". The barangay passes on the warning and runs the evacuation [S16] [S18].
- TY-19: The barangay gives early warning to residents so they can evacuate safely. Barangay tanods patrol risky areas. [S18] "Provide factual and timely information, as well as early warning to the community, for the immediate and safe evacuation of residents, particularly those living in disaster-prone areas." "Assign barangay tanods to conduct patrol within the barangay to monitor flood and landslide-prone areas."
  - Unsafe choice: hiding from or running away from the tanod who comes to warn the family. The tanod's job is to watch risky places and help warn people [S18].
- TY-20: Warning tools used in Philippine communities: sirens, bells (including church bells), the "batingaw", megaphones, public address systems, color-coded flags, radio and text blasts. [S17] "Bells (use of local resources such as church bells is also advisable)" "Siren, batingaw" "Visible warning signs (color-coded flags)" "Public address system" "Megaphone". [S18] barangay equipment list: "Megaphone", "Siren", "Public Address System". [S17] the mayor's warning script can be sent "through the LGU's public address system, broadcast it in local radios, and send it through text blasts." [S16] warnings go out "through diverse mass media, particularly radio, landline communications, and technologies for communication within rural communities".
  - Unsafe choice: thinking the siren or bell is a game or a holiday, and going out to look. It is a warning; tell a Grown-up at once.
- TY-21: Before PAGASA raises a signal, LGUs get "Critical Preparedness Action" alerts: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie (Charlie strongest). At Bravo, pre-emptive or mandatory evacuation may start. At Charlie it must be done before landfall. [S17] "CPAs are issued before PAGASA hoists a tropical cyclone warning signal" and (Bravo) "Critical preparedness actions such as preemptive or mandatory evacuation may be implemented, if necessary." and (Charlie) "They also must implement preemptive or mandatory evacuation to ensure that all families in high and medium risk areas are safe before the typhoon or cyclone landfall."
  - Unsafe choice: waiting for the storm to arrive before leaving a high-risk home. Evacuation is meant to finish before landfall [S17].

## 4. Preparing (before)

- TY-22: Listen to weather news on radio or TV, and follow PAGASA updates. [S15] (DepEd Grade 4, "What to Do Before a Typhoon") "1. Listen to weather forecasts." [S4] "Listen to the radio or watch the news for the latest updates." [S8] "always be on the updates of the tropical cyclone advisories, alerts or warnings which PAGASA disseminates". [S25] (in plain words) have a battery-powered radio.
  - Unsafe choice: playing games or watching cartoons and not telling a Grown-up about the typhoon news.
- TY-23: Pack a Go Bag (emergency kit). Philippine lists include: drinking water, ready-to-eat food, a flashlight with extra batteries, a whistle, a radio, a first aid kit and medicine, a charged power bank, a raincoat, a blanket, clothes, emergency numbers, important documents and some money. [S20] (in plain words) water, food that does not spoil, first aid and medicine, a crank or battery flashlight, a charged power bank, a whistle for each family member including the children, a raincoat, rope, a portable radio, a blanket, a notebook and pencil, a list of emergency numbers including the barangay and LGU, a toy or comfort object for children, documents and cash. [S17] (mayor's script, Filipino) the Go bag/"Emergency Balde" holds ready-to-eat food like biscuits and canned goods, clothes, "flashlight", medicine, money, family documents and bottled water. [S21] (OCD list, in plain words) a flashlight, a whistle, a radio with batteries, a first aid kit, cash, ready-to-eat food for three days, water in sealed bottles, phones, power banks and chargers.
  - Note: some lists also include candles and matches [S20] [S21] [S15]. See TY-38 and the conflicts list.
  - Unsafe choice: packing only toys and snacks and leaving out water and the flashlight. (A comfort toy is fine; UNICEF lists one [S20].)
- TY-24: Keep the Go Bag ready and easy to grab. Check it often. [S20] (in plain words) the bag should be waterproof, light enough for any family member to carry, always complete, and kept where it can be grabbed quickly. [S21] (OCD, in plain words) check and refill it every three months. [S25] (in plain words) a Go-Kit holds 3 days of supplies you can carry.
  - Unsafe choice: hiding the Go Bag under many boxes or taking things out of it to play. It must be complete and quick to grab [S20].
- TY-25: Charge phones and power banks before the typhoon. [S22] "Keep your cell phone charged" [S19] (Filipino, in plain words) make sure the go-bag is complete and the phone and power bank are fully charged. [S21] lists "power banks, and chargers" in the Go Bag.
  - Unsafe choice: using up the phone battery on games the night before the storm. Power may be out for days [S1] [S25].
- TY-26: Store water and food that need no cooking. [S15] "3. Store adequate supply of ready-to-eat food, clean water, and clothing." [S27] "Store drinking water in jugs and bottles." [S25] (in plain words) be ready to live without power and water for a long time.
  - Unsafe choice: drinking up the stored water as play or leaving the water jugs uncovered.
- TY-27: Have flashlights and extra batteries ready. [S23] "Have flashlights for every household member." [S27] "At least two waterproof flashlights with extra, fresh batteries" [S17] lists "Flashlight and extra batteries". [S15] "Prepare flashlights, batteries" (the module also lists candles and kerosene lamps; see TY-38).
  - Unsafe choice: using the flashlight as a toy until the batteries die before the storm.
- TY-28: Bring in or tie down loose things outside, because strong wind throws them. [S22] "bring in outside furniture" [S27] "Anchor outside objects that cannot be brought inside" [S15] "Prepare for wind. Secure yard items that may blow around." [S25] (in plain words) secure outside items that high winds could pick up and use to hurt someone. [S19] (Filipino, in plain words) tie down the roof and other things the wind could carry away. A child helps a Grown-up with light things only. (The "light things only" limit is a game rule, not a source.)
  - Unsafe choice: leaving the bike, pails and plastic chairs in the yard. Signal 2 winds make light things fly (TY-03).
- TY-29: Grown-ups fix the house and trim branches that could fall. [S15] "2. Check and fix your house for any damage or unstable parts." [S19] (Filipino, in plain words) check the roof, doors and windows and fix them; cut tree branches that may fall. [S18] barangays "Assist in the pruning of trees and vegetation and clearing of waterways."
  - Unsafe choice: a child climbing the roof or a tree to "help". This is Grown-up work. (No source says "children must not climb"; the sources give the task to adults and the barangay.)
- TY-30: Know where the evacuation center is and how to get there. [S15] "8. Know the location of evacuation centers in town." [S8] learn the locations of "concrete reinforced multi-story buildings designated by the local government for evacuation" and the safest, quickest route to high ground. [S25] (in plain words) know where you will go and how you will get there.
  - Unsafe choice: not knowing the way, then taking a shortcut through the river or canal during evacuation (TY-52).
- TY-31: Grown-ups explain the typhoon to children so they are ready and not frightened. [S19] (Filipino, in plain words) explain to children how to be careful and prepare so they are not afraid of the typhoon; during the storm, let children feel there is nothing to fear because the family is ready.

## Why the wrong choices are unsafe

- Going outside to play or watch during the storm: winds throw things, and people out in strong wind can be hurt (TY-03, TY-05, TY-06, TY-32, TY-46).
- Standing near a window: windows can blow out at Signal 3 and 4; stay in an inside room (TY-04, TY-05, TY-33).
- Taping a window and staying near it: tape gives no protection (TY-38).
- Leaving loose things in the yard: Signal 2 wind makes them fly (TY-03, TY-28).
- Packing only toys in the Go Bag: water, food, flashlight and radio are needed for days without power (TY-23, TY-26, TY-40).
- Using up the phone battery: power can be out for days (TY-25, TY-40).
- Lighting a candle in a power outage: open flames start fires; use a flashlight (TY-39; FI-60, FI-61).
- Opening the refrigerator again and again: food stays cold only about 4 hours (TY-41).
- Walking home alone when classes stop: the school keeps learners if travel is unsafe (TY-13, TY-36).
- Going to school at Signal 2: face-to-face classes for Grades 3 to 6 are already suspended (TY-12).
- Going out in the eye of the storm: the other side of the storm can come back in minutes (TY-37).
- Ignoring the siren, bell or megaphone: it is the barangay's early warning (TY-19, TY-20).
- Staying behind or going back for a toy when told to evacuate: evacuate right away (TY-51, TY-53).
- Taking a shortcut across a creek or flooded field: shortcuts may be blocked; moving water knocks you down (TY-48, TY-52).
- Watching the waves at the beach: storm surge floods low coasts (TY-10, TY-50).
- Touching or poking a fallen wire, or stepping in water near it: it can electrocute; water near it can carry electricity (TY-44, TY-45).
- Picking up fruit or branches during the storm: flying debris and falling limbs (TY-46).
- Playing or wading in floodwater: it is fast, dirty and can make you sick (TY-47, TY-48, TY-49).
- Drinking tap or puddle water after the typhoon: water may be contaminated; drink boiled or safe water (TY-58).
- Going home or out before the all-clear: wait for officials (TY-55).
- Exploring a damaged house: damaged houses must be checked first (TY-57).
- Helping with cleanup: children do not do cleanup work (TY-59).

DO 22, s. 2024 [S13] and EO 66, s. 2012 [S14]: Signal 1 suspends only Kindergarten (pre-school); Signal 2 suspends face-to-face classes from Kindergarten to Grade 10 (EO 66: pre-school, elementary and secondary); Signal 3 or higher suspends all levels. DO 22 is newer and says "All provisions of other existing issuances inconsistent with this Order are repealed, rescinded, or modified accordingly." DO 22 does not name DO 37 in the pages read. Brigada News says DO 37 is repealed; an Inquirer report called the new order a complement (both SECONDARY ONLY). Use DO 22: for Grade 3 to 6, Signal 2 or higher. The DepEd Grade 4 module [S15] (2020) also says Signal 1 = preschool, Signal 2 = preschool to high school, Signal 3 = all levels.
2. **Yellow rainfall warning.** DO 37 [S12] and DO 22 [S13] both leave yellow to the local chief executive, and make orange and red automatic. 2022 news reports (GMA, SECONDARY ONLY) said yellow also suspends classes automatically. Use the orders.
3. **Candles vs flashlight.** American Red Cross [S26] (use flashlights, not candles), NOAA [S27] ("avoid open flames, such as candles and kerosene lamps"), NFPA and BFP (FI-60, FI-61) say flashlight. UNICEF Philippines' go-bag list [S20] includes candles and matches; the OCD list via PTV [S21] has "candles, matches"; DepEd's Grade 4 module [S15] says "Prepare flashlights, batteries, candles, and kerosene lamps." The game should show only the flashlight, and never a child with a candle or matches.
4. **Windows: tape, cloth or shutters.** NOAA [S27]: taping gives "absolutely no protection"; use shutters or plywood. American Red Cross [S25]: shutters or plywood. DepEd Grade 4 [S15]: "Cover your glass windows and appliances with cloth to avoid splinters when they break." No source tested cloth. All sources agree: stay away from windows (TY-33). The game should not show taping or covering as the Safe Choice.
5. **Open vs closed windows.** NOAA [S27] before the storm: "Secure buildings by closing and boarding up windows". NOAA after the storm, if the home is damaged: "open windows and doors to ventilate and dry your home." NOAA, if you smell gas: "open a window and quickly leave the building." No source read says to open windows during a typhoon (the "open a window to equalize pressure" idea was not found in any source). Keep windows closed during the storm in the game.
6. **Which signal system.** PAGASA [S1] [S2]: 5 levels since 23 March 2022 (39-61, 62-88, 89-117, 118-184, 185+ km/h). DepEd Grade 4 module [S15] (2020) teaches 4 levels with other speeds ("60 kph", "more than 60 kph but not more than 100kph", "more than 100 kph up to 185 kph", "more than 185 kph"). The PAGASA poster [S6] shows an older 5-level system with Super Typhoon "more than 220 kph". Use [S1].
7. **When to evacuate.** UNICEF Philippines [S19]: evacuate at once when PAGASA, the LGU or the barangay gives a warning. PAGASA [S8]: prepare to evacuate when the MDRRMC or barangay officials advise, and evacuate "when called for mandatory evacuation." DILG [S17]: the LGU decides pre-emptive or mandatory evacuation by Alpha/Bravo/Charlie level. PAGASA rainfall flyer [S4]: red warning = "Evacuate to higher ground". DepEd Grade 4 [S15] (old Signal 3): "People from low-lying areas must evacuate." For the game: go with the Grown-ups when the barangay or officials say to go.
8. **When it is safe to go outside or home.** NOAA [S27], Ready.gov [S24] and American Red Cross [S25]: only when authorities say it is safe. UNICEF Philippines [S19]: listen to the news and make sure it is safe outside (family judgment). DepEd [S15] says "Monitor the movement of the typhoon." No source says the calm in the eye is safe (TY-37). For the game: wait for the officials or the radio.
9. **Go Bag duration.** American Red Cross [S25]: Go-Kit for 3 days. OCD via PTV [S21]: ready-to-eat food for three days; check every three months. UNICEF [S20]: no number of days. NOAA [S27]: water "for up to seven days". CONTEXT.md's Go Bag says 3 days. Keep 3 days.
10. **Boiling time for drinking water.** News reports quote DOH as "at least two minutes" (2017, PCO) and "3 minutes" (older). SECONDARY ONLY. DepEd [S15] and PAGASA [S7] say "Boil water" with no time. The game should say "boiled water" with no time.
