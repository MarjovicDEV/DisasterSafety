## 1. What a landslide is

- LS-01: A landslide is soil, rock and debris moving down a slope. Heavy rain and earthquakes set it off. [S1] "A landslide is the mass movement of rock, soil, and debris down a slope due to gravity." Natural triggers: "Intense rainfall", "Weathering of rocks", "Ground vibrations created during earthquakes", "Volcanic activity". [S5] "This can be caused by continuous heavy rains (rain-induced landslides) or shaking due to earthquake (earthquake-induced landslides)." [S4] "Water is the primary trigger for landslides."
- LS-02: A landslide can be slow or fast, and can reach places below and away from the slope. [S1] "The movement may range from very slow to rapid. It can affect areas both near and far from the source." [S6] "The most dangerous, life-threatening and deadliest landslides are the ones that occur quickly, often with little notice."
  - Unsafe choice: thinking "the slope is far up, so its foot is safe".
- LS-03: Steep slopes and slopes with gullies are the ones prone to slide. [S4] checklist: "Steep Slope Gradient: The slope is very steep, estimated to be roughly 26 degrees or higher." "Presence of Gulleys: The area has natural channels, ravines, or "gulleys" where water concentrates." See also EQ-82.

## 2. Warning signs (Slide Signs)

- LS-04: New cracks in the ground. [S3] "new cracks or unusual bulges in the ground, street pavements or sidewalks". [S4] "Tension Cracks: Visible open cracks on the ground, usually running parallel to the slope." [S6] "Slowly developing, widening cracks appear on the ground or on paved areas such as streets or driveways."
  - Unsafe choice: jumping over or playing beside a new crack instead of telling a Grown-up.
- LS-05: Trees, posts and fences that lean. [S3] "leaning telephone poles, trees, retaining walls or fences". [S4] ""Drunken" Trees or Poles: Trees, fences, or utility poles are tilting or bending (J-shaped trunks), indicating Soil Creep."
  - Unsafe choice: climbing or playing under a tree that leans more than before.
- LS-06: New springs, seeps, muddy springs and wet ground. [S3] "springs, seeps, or saturated ground in areas that have not typically been wet before". [S4] "New or Muddy Springs: Sudden appearance of water springs where there were none, or existing clear springs turning muddy/turbid." [S7] (in plain words) water in rivers and streams turning from clear to muddy is a sign. [S6] "water that changes from clear to muddy".
  - Unsafe choice: splashing in, or going up to look at, muddy water coming out of a slope.
- LS-07: Cracks and tilting in the house. [S3] "soil moving away from foundations"; "tilting or cracking of concrete floors and foundations"; "broken water lines and other underground utilities". [S6] "Doors or windows stick or jam for the first time." "New cracks appear in plaster, tile, brick or foundations."
  - Unsafe choice: hiding or ignoring a new crack in the wall.
- LS-08: Bulging ground at the foot of a slope, and cracked or sinking walls. [S4] "Damaged/Sinking Ripraps or Retaining Walls"; "Bulging: The ground at the foot (toe) of the slope appears swollen or raised (heaving)." [S6] "Bulging ground appears at the base of a slope."
- LS-09: Sounds. [S4] (question the inspectors ask residents) "Have you heard any rumbling sounds or snapping roots?" [S6] "Unusual sounds such as trees cracking or boulders knocking together, might indicate moving debris. A faint rumbling sound that increases in volume is noticeable as the landslide nears." [S7] (in plain words) unusual sounds like rocks colliding, trees breaking or noises underground.
- LS-10: Report the signs. Residents are the barangay's eyes. [S4] "The LGU depends on "Human Sensors" (Barangay officials/residents) to visually spot observable indicators such as: New cracks on the ground or structures (concrete floors, ripraps). Leaning trees, poles, or fences (indicating Creep). Murky water in springs or sudden changes in water flow." The inspectors "shall interview long-time residents or Barangay Tanods." [S7] (in plain words) if you notice signs, tell local authorities and the people around you at once. See also TY-19 (tanods patrol "flood and landslide-prone areas").
  - Child's action (game adaptation): tell a Grown-up at once. No source names what a child does.

## 3. Before: habits and plans

- LS-11: Know the landslide-prone places, the signs, the evacuation site and the safest way there. Keep a Go Bag. [S5] "Know the landslide prone areas and learn the early signs of impending landslides." "Prepare your family's Emergency Go Bag containing items needed for survival." "Know the location of the evacuation site and the fastest and safest way to go there." [S7] (in plain words) practise the evacuation route with the family and agree a meeting point; learn the school's plan.
- LS-12: People weaken slopes by cutting or digging them and clearing their plants. [S4] "Unprotected Excavation: Slopes that have been cut (for road widening or house building) without retaining walls." "Slope Toe Modification: Removal of soil/rock at the bottom of the slope, removing its support." "Deforestation/Kaingin: Burnt or cleared vegetation on steep slopes."
  - Unsafe choice (game adaptation): digging into the foot of the slope to play. No source names children digging.
- LS-13: Plants, drainage and walls protect slopes. [S2] the "Engineering" tab shows photos titled benching, retaining walls, riprap, gabion walls, shotcrete, drainage, erosion control and vetiver grass. Photos only; no text. WEAK.
- LS-14: Landslide hazard areas get posted signs. [S2] the "Landslide Warning Signs" photo shows a board on a slope reading "LANDSLIDE HAZARD ZONE", posted by a municipal DRRM council. [S4] when inspectors see active movement they "Cordon off the area." [S7] (in plain words) do not try to pass barricades that responders set up.
  - Unsafe choice: going past the sign or the rope to get a ball or to play.

## 4. Warnings and evacuation

- LS-15: Warnings follow the PAGASA rainfall colours. [S4] "Yellow Warning (Monitor): Monitor the weather condition. Warn residents in low-lying and landslide-prone areas to prepare. Orange Warning (Alert): Prepare for evacuation. Landslide risk is elevated; residents should be on high alert for ground movement. Red Warning (Evacuation): Immediate evacuation of communities in hazard zones (flood and landslide areas)." See also TY-09 (Orange "Possible landslides"; Red "Evacuate to higher ground and away from landslide prone areas").
- LS-16: The barangay carries the warning to each house. [S4] "The LGU cascades warnings received from National Agencies (OCD, PAGASA) down to the Barangay level." "The LGU uses traditional methods like house-to-house warnings, church bells/sirens, and social media blasts based on the color-coded rainfall warning." See also TY-19 and TY-20 (megaphone).
- LS-17: When local officials say evacuate, go at once. [S5] "When notified by local officials, immediately evacuate to safer grounds." [S4] "Pre-emptive Evacuation - The voluntary relocation of residents from high-risk zones, highly encouraged by the LCE, usually during an Orange Alert"; "Mandatory Evacuation - The non-negotiable relocation of residents from high-risk zones, enforced by the Local Chief Executive (LCE), usually during a Red Alert or when an active slide is confirmed." [S6] "by the time you are sure a debris flow is coming, it will be too late to get away safely." See also TY-51.
  - Unsafe choice: waiting for the rain to stop, or for the slope to move, before leaving.
- LS-18: Classes can stop for landslide risk, and the school keeps learners when the way home is unsafe. TY-14 (DO 22, s. 2024: school heads may suspend classes "In case of torrential rain, flooding, and risk of landslides and storm surges"; learners stay "if traveling has become unsafe"). TY-13.

## 5. During heavy rain or a slide

- LS-19: Outdoors, keep away from the slide and its path; do not cross damaged roads. [S5] "When outside, avoid affected areas and go to a safer place." "When driving, do not cross bridges and damaged roads." [S6] "Never cross a road with water or mud flowing." [S6] "If you do get stuck in the path of a landslide move uphill as quickly as possible." (Not used: no Level puts a person in the path.)
  - Unsafe choice: crossing mud that runs over the road below a slope.
- LS-20: Indoors when leaving is not possible, stay in and get under a sturdy table; protect the head. [S5] "When inside a house or building and evacuation is not possible, stay inside and get under a sturdy table." "When landslide cannot be avoided, protect your head." Not used in the game (design L2: no Level shows a slide reaching a house with a person in it).
- LS-21: Stay away from slopes, streams and low places during heavy rain. [S7] (in plain words) stay away from risky areas; do not wade streams, gather firewood or fish during heavy rain or when there are unusual signs. [S6] "Avoid river valleys and low-lying areas during times of danger." See also FL-12.
- LS-22: Heavy rain at night is a danger time. [S6] "Stay alert and awake during a storm that could cause a landslide. Many deaths from landslides occur while people are sleeping." [S7] (in plain words) stay alert during long heavy rain, especially at night. Not used (too frightening for the Learners).

## 6. After

- LS-23: The ground stays dangerous after the rain stops. [S4] "Saturated Ground: The ground remains boggy or wet even days after rain." [S6] "Stay away from the slide area. There may be danger coming from additional slides."
  - Unsafe choice: playing on the wet slope as soon as the rain stops.
- LS-24: Leave the evacuation area only when the authorities say it is safe. [S5] "Leave the evacuation area only when authorities say it is safe." [S7] (in plain words) return home only when it is officially declared safe. See also TY-55.
- LS-25: Keep away from the place where the landslide happened. [S5] "Avoid landslide affected areas." [S6] (quoted in LS-23).
  - Unsafe choice: climbing on the fallen soil or picking things out of it.
- LS-26: Floods can follow a landslide. [S5] "Watch out for possible flashfloods due to clogging of creeks or rivers." [S6] "Watch for flooding. Floods sometimes follow landslides and debris flows because they may both be started by the same conditions."
- LS-27: Report fallen trees and posts; let trained people check the house and land. [S5] "Report fallen trees and electric posts to proper authorities." "Check your house for possible damages and repair as necessary." [S6] "Allow trained professionals to check the building foundation, chimney, and surrounding land for damage."
- LS-28: Children do not do the cleanup. [S7] (in plain words) limit children's part in cleaning up as much as possible. See also TY-59 (Ready.gov). No Philippine source read.
  - Unsafe choice: shovelling the mud with the Grown-ups.

## 7. Earthquake-triggered landslides

- LS-29: Earthquakes set off landslides; after shaking, move away from steep slopes. [S1] trigger "Ground vibrations created during earthquakes"; example "Earthquake-induced rockslide, Brgy. Conalum, Argao, Cebu, October 2013 – M 7.2 Bohol Earthquake". [S5] (quoted in LS-01). [S4] the Red Alert trigger includes "Earthquake Intensity VII+", and slope checks are done "Post- Earthquake". EQ-82 "Move away from steep slopes which may be affected by landslides." The action during the shaking stays "Duck, Cover, and Hold" (CONTEXT.md).
  - Unsafe choice: after the shaking, waiting by the slope or under the tree on it.
