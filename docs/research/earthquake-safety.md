# Earthquake Safety for Grade 3-6 Learners (Philippines)

Date: 2026-11-16

Purpose: Source-backed earthquake safety facts for the game's Earthquake levels (first use: Easy / Level 1, classroom shaking). Every finding has a stable ID (EQ-xx) so game text can cite it.

## How this was gathered

- Primary sources only: PHIVOLCS (DOST), DepEd, NDRRMC, OCD.
- PHIVOLCS flyers and posters are linked from the PHIVOLCS "Earthquake Preparedness" page and hosted on Google Drive. They are image files. Quotes from them were read from the rendered image, not from extracted text. Spelling and capitals are kept as printed.
- DepEd PDFs were downloaded and their text was extracted. DO 53, s. 2022 is a scanned PDF with poor text recognition, so only clear lines are used.
- ocd.gov.ph and ndrrmc.gov.ph could not be fetched (HTTP 403, plus a bot check on ocd.gov.ph). No OCD or NDRRMC lines are quoted as primary. See the last section.
- For Normal (EQ-52 onward), more PHIVOLCS flyers and infographics were read as images. Some Google Drive files block download; those were read from the Drive preview image (see the last section).

## 1. Official protective-action wording

- EQ-01: The PHIVOLCS-DepEd school primer uses "DUCK, COVER and HOLD" for classroom shaking. [S1] "Get under a sturdy table/desk and do the "DUCK, COVER and HOLD""
- EQ-02: The PHIVOLCS school drill primer uses "duck, cover and hold" for the drill response. [S2] "While the alarm is ongoing, everyone should perform "duck, cover and hold"."
- EQ-03: The PHIVOLCS public poster uses "DUCK, COVER and HOLD". [S3] "Do the "DUCK, COVER and HOLD"." The Filipino poster keeps the English phrase. [S4] "Gawin ang "DUCK, COVER and HOLD"."
- EQ-04: PHIVOLCS NSED news uses "Duck, Cover, and Hold". [S7] "prompts everyone to do the Duck, Cover, and Hold" and [S8] "performed the Duck, Cover, and Hold technique and evacuated the building"
- EQ-05: Recent PHIVOLCS earthquake primers use "Drop, Cover, and Hold" instead. [S5] (2026) "the public is advised to protect themselves by doing the "Drop, Cover, and Hold" procedure." [S6] (2019) "doing the "drop, cover and hold" if inside a structurally sound building."
- EQ-06: DepEd Order No. 27, s. 2015 uses "Duck-Cover-Hold" and also "Duck-Hold-Cover (DCH)". [S9] "the initial response during an earthquake (Duck-Cover-Hold)?" and "Duck-Hold-Cover (DCH) as an initial response during earthquakes"
- EQ-07: A DepEd Grade 10 activity sheet uses a fourth form. [S11] "Earthquake: Drop, Under, Cover, Hold On"
- EQ-08: DepEd regional NSED reporting (2026) uses "Duck, Cover, and Hold". [S12] "followed the standard safety protocol, "Duck, Cover, and Hold,""
- EQ-09: Recommended game wording: **"Duck, Cover, and Hold"**. Reasons: (a) it is the term in both school-specific PHIVOLCS primers made with or for DepEd [S1][S2]; (b) DepEd's own order uses it [S9]; (c) it is the term used in NSED drills that Grade 3-6 learners already practice [S8][S12]. "Drop, Cover, and Hold" in recent PHIVOLCS primers [S5] means the same action. The game could show it once as an alternate name, for example in a teacher or parent note. Avoid "Duck-Hold-Cover" and "Drop, Under, Cover, Hold On". Each appears in one document only.

## 2. What a learner does in the classroom during shaking

- EQ-10: Get under a sturdy table or desk and stay there until the shaking stops. [S1] "Get under a sturdy table/desk ... Stay put until the shaking stops."
- EQ-11: Hold on to the desk or table. If you cannot, protect your head with your arms. [S3] "Duck under a sturdy desk or table, and hold on to it, or protect your head with your arms."
- EQ-12: Hold on to your cover until the shaking stops. [S2] (picture caption) "Take cover under a sturdy table and hold to your cover until the shaking stops"
- EQ-13: Stay in the position until the shaking stops. [S2] "Remain in this position until the "shaking" stops."
- EQ-14: Stay away from glass windows, shelves, cabinets and heavy objects. [S3] "Stay away from glass windows, shelves, cabinets and other heavy objects." [S2] "Keep away from glass window and heavy shelves"
- EQ-15: Watch out for falling objects. [S2] "Watch out for falling objects" [S3] "Beware of falling objects. Be alert and keep your eyes open."
- EQ-16: Inside a structurally sound building, stay inside. [S3] "When you are INSIDE a structurally sound building or home...STAY THERE!"
- EQ-17: A door can be opened for the exit, by an assigned learner in drills. [S3] "If possible quickly open the door for exit." [S2] "Assigned student in-charge of making sure the door is open during shaking"

## 3. Why the wrong choices are unsafe

### Running outside

- EQ-18: PHIVOLCS says to stay inside a sound building during shaking, not run out. [S3] "When you are INSIDE a structurally sound building or home...STAY THERE!"
- EQ-19: Learners move only after the shaking stops. [S1] "As soon as the shaking stops... Leave the classroom immediately." [S2] "As soon as the shaking stops, immediately evacuate the school building"
- EQ-20: Running is not allowed, even during evacuation. [S1] (instructions to students) "WALK. DO NOT RUN. DO NOT PUSH. DO NOT TALK."
- Note: No primary source found that gives the reason in words (for example, falling while running, or debris at exits). See "Unsourced or uncertain".

### Standing by the window

- EQ-21: Windows and glass panes are listed as unsafe zones in school. [S2] "Some of the unsafe zones: Windows and glass panes"
- EQ-22: Broken window glass is a falling-object danger. [S1] "Stay away from falling objects such as pieces of broken glass windows, ceiling fans, etc."
- EQ-23: Strong shaking can injure people through broken glass. [S1] "Strong ground shaking can cause injuries to people due to broken glasses and fallen objects."
- EQ-24: Window panes may break during shaking. [S1] "window panes may rattle and may even break."

### Panicking, crying, freezing

- EQ-25: Stay calm. [S3] "STAY CALM." [S4] "MAGING MAHINAHON." [S2] "Keep calm and don't panic"
- EQ-26: Panic stops clear thinking. [S9] "Panic could result to inability to think during emergencies"
- EQ-27: Do not panic after the shaking either. [S3] (under "AFTER ... Don't...") "...PANIC."
- EQ-28: Protect yourself first. [S1] "When a strong shaking starts... Protect yourself."
- Note: "Cry and stay still" is partly wrong, not fully wrong. Staying still in an open spot does not protect the head from falling objects (EQ-15, EQ-22). No primary source mentions crying or freezing by name.

## 4. Falling-object hazards in classrooms and schools

- EQ-29: Falling objects to avoid include broken window glass and ceiling fans. [S1] "Stay away from falling objects such as pieces of broken glass windows, ceiling fans, etc."
- EQ-30: School hazard list from the PHIVOLCS-DepEd primer. [S1]
  - "Large and heavy pieces of furniture that could topple, such as book cases and cabinets;"
  - "Hanging flower pots and ceiling fans;"
  - "Glass window panes and walls that could break;"
  - "Heavy picture frames and mirrors;"
  - "Latches on cabinets that will not hold cabinet door closed;"
  - "Glass or other breakable objects that are kept on high and open shelves;"
  - "Obstructions such as plant boxes, chairs and benches, bulletin boards, trash cans that may block exit points and corridors; and"
  - "Elevated water tanks and electrical posts."
- EQ-31: Things that move or fall during shaking. [S1] "hanging objects like lamps, wall frames swing violently or may even fall down." and "bookshelves, cabinets, tables and chairs may move, be shifted and/or fall over."
- EQ-32: Unsafe zones in school include shelves and furniture that can topple. [S2] "Book shelves, machinery, cabinets and furniture that may topple or slide"
- EQ-33: Hanging objects should be fastened beforehand. [S5] "hanging objects properly fastened to prevent injuries." [S3] "Check the stability of hanging objects like ceiling fans and chandeliers."

## 5. After the shaking stops

- EQ-34: Leave the classroom and building in an orderly way. [S1] "Leave the classroom immediately. Get out of the building in an orderly manner."
- EQ-35: Follow the class marshal or teacher on the set route. [S2] "evacuate the school building and proceed to identified evacuation areas using the pre-determined routes guided by the class marshall or teacher."
- EQ-36: Walk. Do not run, push, or talk. [S1] "WALK. DO NOT RUN. DO NOT PUSH. DO NOT TALK."
- EQ-37: Go to the evacuation area. [S1] "Proceed to the identified evacuation area."
- EQ-38: The evacuation area is an open space near the building. [S2] "Use all available OPEN SPACES nearest to the building"
- EQ-39: At the evacuation area, group by class and teachers count learners. [S2] "students must be grouped together according to the class where they belong." and "Teachers should check and make sure all students are accounted for."
- EQ-40: Wait for the "all clear". [S2] "The over-all coordinator will annouce the termination of drill or "All clear"."
- EQ-41: When to evacuate. [S2] "After a very strong earthquake wherein you lose balance or could not stand, and heavy objects and furniture start to be moved or shifted." and "As need arises or as declared by authorities."
- EQ-42: Expect aftershocks. [S3] "Be prepared for aftershocks." [S5] "We can expect aftershocks in the epicentral area."
- EQ-43: Do not go back into damaged buildings. [S3] "Don't... ...enter damaged buildings." [S9] "we should not go back to our homes/buildings due to possible effects of aftershocks?"
- EQ-44: Outside, stay away from trees, power lines, posts and concrete structures. [S3] "Stay away from trees, powerlines, posts and concrete structures."
- EQ-45: Covering the head while walking out: the [S2] drill primer shows (Phase 3 picture) learners walking out with books held over their heads. This is a picture only. No text says "duck, cover, hold while walking". Treat as uncertain (see last section).
- EQ-46: Schools must hold drills often. [S10] "all public schools are hereby required to conduct unannounced earthquake and fire drills every first and third week of every month." (Clean reading of a scanned page. Words were checked by eye against the garbled text.)

## 6. If no desk is available

- EQ-47: DepEd says Duck, Cover, Hold must also be practiced where there is no table. [S9] "should be practiced in different contexts, especially in areas without tables or any stable furniture to stay under."
- EQ-48: If you cannot hold on to a desk, protect your head with your arms. [S3] "...hold on to it, or protect your head with your arms."
- EQ-49: Use a book to cover your head. [S2] (picture caption) "Use a book to cover your head"
- EQ-50: Hide under an armchair (school armchair desk). [S2] (picture caption) "Hide under an armchair"
- EQ-51: Still stay away from windows, shelves and heavy objects. [S3] "Stay away from glass windows, shelves, cabinets and other heavy objects."

## 7. Before an earthquake: at home and at school

Note: game source IDs must have two digits (`EQ-xx`), so EQ-99 is the last usable ID. Related points are grouped into one finding to save IDs.

### Planning and knowing the place

- EQ-52: Planning ahead is the key to staying safe. Know the earthquake hazards where you live. [S13] "The key to effective disaster prevention is planning:" [S18] "KNOW THE HAZARDS. Familiarize yourself with earthquake hazards in your area."
- EQ-53: Know the exits of your school and your house. [S13] "Familiarize yourself with the exit routes." [S25] "Familiarize yourself with the safety exits and safe area of your school and house."
- EQ-54: Know the evacuation area for home and for school, and the nearest way to get there. [S18] "KNOW THE EVACUATION AREA. Check the nearest route going to identified evacuation area." [S9] "Do we all know where the nearest safe evacuation area from our house is in the event of an earthquake?" [S15] "Know where the school-designated evacuation area."
- EQ-55: Walk the evacuation route with your family before an earthquake. The evacuation area is an open space. [S15] "Walk-through the evacuation route going to the designated evacuation area (open space)"
- EQ-56: Learners are taught that the evacuation route should not pass by things that can fall, like posts and water tanks. [S14] "KASAMA SA MGA ITINUTURO SA MGA MAG-AARAL ANG PAG-IWAS NA MAGING DAANAN PARA SA EVACUATION ROUTE ANG MGA LUGAR NA MAY ISTRUKTURA NA MAAARING BUMAGSAK, KATULAD NG MGA POSTE AT WATER TANK."
- EQ-57: Look for the safe and unsafe spots in your house with your family. [S15] "Conduct observation on safe and unsafe spots in your house." and "Have your family member identify each."
- EQ-58: Know where the fire extinguishers, first aid kits and alarms are. [S13] "Know where fire extinguishers, first aid kits, alarms, and communication facilities are located."

### Making the house and classroom safer

- EQ-59: Heavy furniture and cabinets should be strapped or bolted to the walls. [S13] "Strap or bolt heavy furnitures/cabinets to the walls." [S19] "Secure shelves, appliances, and furniture to the walls or ceiling."
- EQ-60: Check that hanging things, like ceiling fans and mirrors, are secure. [S13] "Check the stability of hanging objects like ceiling fans and chandeliers." [S25] (Do or Don't activity, answer "Do") "Fix mirrors, paintings, and other hanging objects securely."
- EQ-61: Keep breakable and dangerous things on low, secured shelves. Do not keep heavy things on high shelves. [S13] "Breakable items, harmful chemicals and flammable materials should be stored properly in the lowermost secured shelves." [S9] "Are overhead shelves/cabinets at home free of heavy objects that may injure people?"
- EQ-62: Do not hang heavy pictures over beds. Put beds and chairs away from windows. [S25] (Do or Don't activity, answer "Don't") "Hang heavy pictures and other items over beds." and (answer "Do") "Locate beds and chairs away from windows."
- EQ-63: Turn off the gas tank when it is not in use. [S3] "Always turn off the gas tanks when not in use."

### Family plan, meeting place and emergency numbers

- EQ-64: Make a plan of where to meet your family after an earthquake. [S25] "Make a plan of where to meet your family after an earthquake." [S15] "Have an agreed meeting place."
- EQ-65: Agree how the family will find each other, who to call outside your area, how long to wait, and a second evacuation area. [S15] "Agree on how to reunite with family members and plan to communicate" and "Designate a friend or relative outside your area to be contacted" [S9] "If communication is down, how long are we going to wait for each other at the designated meeting place?" and "If the designated evacuation area is damaged, where is our alternative?"
- EQ-66: The family should plan how to help babies, persons with disabilities and the elderly. [S9] "Do we know how to evacuate infants/children, persons with disabilities, and/or the elderly in our house?"
- EQ-67: Know the emergency numbers (police, fire station, hospital, barangay). Each family member should remember or write down at least one family number. [S15] "Know the community emergency contact numbers and facilities" [S9] "Do we know the emergency numbers and contact details of the local fire department, police, hospitals and barangay officials?" and "each member should be able to recall or list down at least one number."

### Emergency supply kit (go bag)

- EQ-68: Prepare an emergency supply kit that lasts 3 days (72 hours). Keep it in a waterproof bag, in a safe place that is easy to reach. [S18] "PREPARE AN EMERGENCY SUPPLY KIT. Make it accessible at all times." [S20] "The supply kit should last at least 3 DAYS or 72 HOURS!" and "It should be kept in water proof bag and in a safe place, and that is easy to find."
- EQ-69: Kit checklist from the PHIVOLCS family guide. [S15]
  - "First aid kit"
  - "Food"
  - "Bottled water"
  - "Flashlight and spare batteries"
  - "Battery-operated radio"
  - "Candles and matches, lighter"
  - "Whistle"
  - "Knife"
  - "Blankets, spare clothes and shoes"
  - "Rope (at least seven (7) meters)"
  - "Toiletries"
  - "Permanent marker, pen and paper"
  - "Emergency contact information"
  - "Important documents"
  - "Cash"
  - (if necessary) "Baby food" and "Medication (those with special needs)"
- EQ-70: A shorter PHIVOLCS kit list. Schools prepare a survival kit too. [S13] "Prepare a handy emergency supply kit with first aid kit, canned food and can opener, water, clothing, blanket, battery-operated radio, flashlights and extra batteries." [S14] "KAMI AY NAGHAHANDA NG EARTHQUAKE SURVIVAL KIT."

### Drills, and who does what

- EQ-71: Join earthquake drills often, at school and at home. [S13] "Conduct and participate in regular earthquake drills." [S18] "PARTICIPATE DURING DRILLS. Regularly practice the evacuation procedure." [S15] "Regularly conduct earthquake drill with your family and other household members."
- EQ-72: Adults lead family preparation: they teach first aid and how to switch off water, gas and electricity, answer DepEd's preparedness questions with the learner, and ask the school about its earthquake rules. [S15] "Teach your family basic first aid, how to use the fire extinguisher, switching off waterlines, gas tanks, and circuit breaker." and "If you have children, inquire with the school administrators about their school policies during earthquakes." [S9] "The parents, guardian/s or any adult family member is requested to answer the following preparedness questions with the learner."
- EQ-73: Families should know if they live near the sea, a lake or a river. Near the coast, choose the high ground and the way to get there beforehand. [S9] "Do we all know if we are near any body of water? E.g. lake, sea, river" [S17] "Pre-determine high ground in your area and identify routes to get there."

## 8. During shaking at home

- EQ-74: Inside a strong house, stay inside and do "Duck, Cover, and Hold". Families should agree on this beforehand. [S13] "When you are INSIDE a structurally sound building or home...STAY THERE!" and "Do the "DUCK, COVER and HOLD"." [S9] "If the earthquake happens while at home, what will be our initial response?"
- EQ-75: Under a sturdy table, or against a column or post of the room, a person is less likely to be hurt. With no table, stay in the safer part of the room. [S24] "a person is less likely to be injured by taking refuge under sturdy tables and against columns or posts of a room during the ground shaking." [S21] "or stay in safer part of the room."
- EQ-76: Watch out for falling objects, glass windows, shelves and cabinets. [S21] "Watch out for falling objects, glass windows, shelves, cabinets, and other heavy objects that may cause injury."
- EQ-77: Do not run when the shaking starts. Do not panic, push or shout. [S25] (Do or Don't activity, answer "Don't") "Run when the shaking starts." and (answer "Don't") "Panic: Run, Push, Talk/Shout."
- EQ-78: Do not rush to the door or into an elevator. [S25] (pretest item marked as what NOT to do) "Rush to the door and get inside an elevator."
- EQ-79: Do not lie down in the "triangle of life" next to furniture. PHIVOLCS does not recommend it. [S24] "DOST-PHIVOLCS does not recommend the void identification technique (triangle of life)"

## 9. During shaking outdoors

### School grounds, street, near buildings

- EQ-80: If you are outside, move to an open area. [S13] "If you're OUTSIDE..move to an open area!" [S21] "IF OUTSIDE. Move to an open area."
- EQ-81: Outside, stay away from trees, power lines, posts, concrete structures and buildings. [S13] "Stay away from trees, powerlines, posts and concrete structures." [S25] (Do or Don't activity, answer "Don't") "Go near buildings, tall trees, power-lines." and "go to an open area away from trees and electric posts."
- EQ-82: Move away from steep slopes. Landslides can happen. [S13] "Move away from steep slopes which may be affected by landslides." [S21] "MOVE AWAY FROM STEEP SLOPES."

### In a vehicle

- EQ-83: In a moving vehicle, stop and get out. Do not cross bridges, overpasses or flyovers. [S13] "If you're in a moving vehicle, STOP and get out!" and "Do not attempt to cross bridges, overpasses, or flyovers which may have been damaged." [S21] "STOP IF INSIDE A MOVING VEHICLE."

### Near the shore (tsunami)

- EQ-84: Near the shore, a strong earthquake means move quickly to higher ground. A tsunami is a series of giant sea waves. [S3] "If you're near the shore and feel an earthquake, especially if it's too strong, move quickly to higher grounds." and "Tsunamis (giant sea waves) might follow." [S17] "A tsunami is not a single wave but a series of waves."
- EQ-85: Three natural signs of a coming local tsunami. [S16]
  - "SHAKE" "A felt earthquake"
  - "DROP" "Unusual sea level change: sudden seawater drop or rise"
  - "ROAR" "Rumbling sound of approaching waves"
- EQ-86: Near the coast, if you notice any of these signs, move to higher ground right away. [S16] "Immediately MOVE to higher ground right after a strong shaking!" [S17] "If unusual sea conditions like rapid lowering of sea level are observed, immediately move towards high grounds."
- EQ-87: Do not stay in low places near the coast. A local tsunami can come too fast for a warning. [S17] "Do not stay in low-lying coastal areas after a strong earthquake." and "There will not be enough time for warning in case of locally generated tsunamis."
- EQ-88: Never go to the beach to watch the waves or to pick up stranded fish. [S17] "Never go down the beach to watch for a tsunami." and "Fishes may be stranded on dry land thereby attracting people to collect them."
- EQ-89: Stay away from beaches and danger areas until an "all clear" is given. [S17] "Stay out of danger areas until an "all clear" is issued by competent authority." [S5] "people should be watchful and stay away from beaches and coasts."

## 10. After the shaking: more steps

- EQ-90: Check yourself and others for injuries. If no one knows first aid, keep the injured person stable and get help. [S13] "Check... yourself and others for injuries." [S22] "CHECK YOURSELF AND OTHERS FOR INJURIES." [S9] "they should at least know how to stabilize the injured person and seek proper assistance from authorities."
- EQ-91: Check for damaged water and electric lines, spilled chemicals, and fires. [S13] "Check... water and electrical lines for damages." and "Check... for spills of chemical, toxic and flammable materials." and "Check... and control fires which may spread." [S22] "PUT-OUT SMALL FIRES USING EXTINGUISHER." [S9] "Is our home free of possible fire-triggers after an earthquake?"
- EQ-92: Once the shaking stops, take the fastest and safest way out. Do not use elevators. Walk fast but do not run or push. [S13] "Once the shaking stops, take the fastest and safest way out of the building." and "Don't... ...use elevators." [S23] "Walk briskly. Do not run." and "Stay calm. Do not push."
- EQ-93: Bring your emergency bag (go bag). If the family leaves the house, leave a message saying where you went. [S23] "bring your emergency bag." [S25] (Do or Don't activity, answer "Do") "Bring your GO BAG with you." [S13] "If you need to evacuate your residence, leave a message stating where you are going and bring your emergency supply kit."
- EQ-94: Stay away from damaged buildings. Do not go back inside until you are told it is safe. [S25] "Stay away from damaged buildings (stuff might fall off the building, or the building could fall on you)" [S23] "Wait for advisory from building management if it is safe to go back." [S6] "it is best to contact the Municipal/City Engineering Office for advice."
- EQ-95: Listen to the radio and wait for official instructions. Use the phone only when needed. Get news from trusted sources so you do not spread false rumors. [S22] "BE UPDATED. Monitor the situation from the radio." [S25] "Turn on the radio. Wait for instructions from the authorized government agency." [S13] "Don't... ...use telephones unless necessary." [S9] "Do we know from whom we can get accurate and credible data/information so as not to contribute to false rumors which may cause panic, etc.?"
